# NUCU Startup Ecosystem Sites — 2026

The archived NUCU 2026 guide describes **Google for Startups, Techstars Tokyo, STATION Ai, Tongali and PREVENT** as complementary examples of entrepreneurship support and healthcare innovation. The profiles below preserve the guide's claims and proposed learning connections, not verified current membership figures, services or organizational roles. Source pages 3, 8, 13, 22–23 and 31–32 support this account.

## Tokyo startup ecosystem

**Google for Startups** and **Techstars Tokyo** introduce entrepreneurship, investment, mentorship and scaling in Japan and internationally. The May 15 schedule names **Sunshine Burri** at Google for Startups (10:30–12:00) and **Rene Budeck** at Techstars Tokyo (14:00–15:00). The guide does not provide confirmed venue addresses or complete session transcripts.

## STATION Ai

The guide describes **STATION Ai in Aichi** as Japan's largest innovation terminal, with **more than 700 startups, partner companies, venture capital/support organizations and universities from Japan and abroad** participating. This is a combined participant-category claim; it must not be restated as “700 startups.” “Largest” and membership totals are historical source claims, not independently verified metrics.

The facility connects startups with established industries in Aichi and the Tokai region. It names event spaces and offices, a member fitness gym and tech lab, plus a public cafe/restaurant, hotel, event space and **Aichi Founders' Hall**. Three business areas are listed: startup/business-creation and growth support through programs/events; maintenance and management of the facility; and management of funds investing in STATION Ai members.

The May 19 plan places an **Aichi Prefecture briefing at 10:00**, **Tongali overview at 10:30**, both in the **3F Central Lounge**, and a **STATION Ai tour at 11:30**.

## Tongali and Dr. Sawako Tanaka

The guide calls **Tongali** a Nagoya University entrepreneurship-education and startup-support initiative for Central Japan's academic communities, launched in **2016**. It describes funding/support for university-led startups, incubation that translates research and ideas into businesses, **tuition-free entrepreneurship education**, co-working spaces, makers' studios with **3D printers**, and networks of mentors, investors and industry leaders. Collaboration with other universities/research institutions broadens this support.

**Dr. Sawako Tanaka** is identified as a Research Administrator in Academic Research & Industry-Academia-Government Collaboration, involved in Tongali. The guide describes her role organizing entrepreneurship workshops involving international students and local residents to identify urban-renewal challenges and develop potential solutions. It gives no date or measured outcomes for those workshops.

## PREVENT Inc.

The guide describes **PREVENT Inc.**, also styled “Prevent,” as a Nagoya health-tech startup shifting healthcare from treatment toward prevention. Its stated aims are preventing lifestyle-related diseases and reducing hospital readmissions, particularly for older people.

Named chronic-condition examples are **diabetes, heart disease and stroke**. Data-driven coaching and remote support are described as personalized post-discharge care developed with **hospitals, local governments and insurance providers**, including **phone/app check-ins, behavior modification and health monitoring**. The approach combines **medical research, behavioral science and digital technology**, extending care beyond hospitals. Reduced costs and improved long-term outcomes are stated aims, not independently established results.

The May 19 itinerary names a **15:30 PREVENT session by Mr. Hagiwara**, in Meeting Room No. 2, Basic Medicine Building. The source does not specify his full name or an attendee roster.
