# NUCU Rural Healthcare and Cultural Sites — 2026

The archived NUCU 2026 guide uses **Nakatsugawa, Agi Clinic, Iwamura Castle Town and Cyclamen elderly care** to connect aging, population decline, healthcare access, mobility and community sustainability. It plans the rural field trip for **May 21, 2026**, while retaining proposal wording in its background section. Source pages 9, 13 and 19–26 do not establish that every activity occurred or describe current services.

## Dr. Nobutaro Ban and Agi Clinic

The guide identifies **Dr. Nobutaro Ban** as director of **Nakatsugawa City National Health Insurance Agi Clinic**, Gifu Prefecture, specializing in community healthcare and primary care. It describes his work with rural/aging communities, continuous comprehensive care, Nagoya University medical-student mentoring, study in the United States and fluent English. These are the source's biographical claims.

**Agi Clinic** is described as a community facility under Nakatsugawa City's National Health Insurance system, serving older and rural residents with limited urban medical access. Named services include **home medical care, preventive medicine, geriatric medicine, chronic-disease management and palliative care**. Its role as a training site gives Nagoya University students direct community-medicine/primary-care experience. The source presents this combination as a model of patient-centered, sustainable rural care.

The 2026 plan places Dr. Ban's lecture at 10:00 and the clinic tour at 10:45, followed by Iwamura and an elderly-care visit. The background frames rural fieldwork as direct observation of labor shortages, cultural erosion and social instability, to support meaningful technology-based ideas.

## Nakatsugawa City

The guide locates Nakatsugawa in southeastern **Gifu Prefecture**, as a gateway between **Chubu and Kanto**. It describes mountains, traditional culture and a former **post town (juku) on the Nakasendo**, connecting **Edo (Tokyo) and Kyoto during the Edo period**. Named examples include **Magome-juku** and **kuri kinton**, a chestnut confection.

The guide connects local history and cultural preservation with sustainable rural development, healthcare access and regional revitalization. Its approximate “about an hour” from Nagoya differs from the itinerary's 08:00–09:45 bus allocation; neither should be presented as a verified current journey time.

## Iwamura Castle Town and Cyclamen

**Iwamura Castle Town is in Ena City, Gifu Prefecture**, not identified by the guide as part of Nakatsugawa City. It developed around **Iwamura Castle**, described as one of Japan's highest mountain castles. Traditional wooden buildings, merchant houses, sake breweries and small shops illustrate a living community connecting heritage, tourism and sustainability.

The castle is associated with **Lady Otsuya**, a female castle ruler. The guide says much of the original structure is gone but stone walls and the mountain setting remain. Students were to consider population decline, cultural preservation and sustainable development alongside medical and mobility needs.

The itinerary names **Cyclamen Elder Care Center / Cyclamen Elderly care house**, with a 14:00 tour and interviews. No private resident or patient information is supplied. The guide does not provide an exact address, confirmed interview protocol or completed findings.
