# NUCU Student Venture Portfolio — 2026

The archived 2026 Leeds Build in Japan article names five student venture concepts developed through international teamwork and field research: **Kizuki Care, Akira Care, Kizuna Village, Anshin Care and Kizuna**. Their intended users span healthcare providers, businesses, local governments, older adults, caregivers, families and elder-care facilities. The article describes startup-style concepts; it does not establish commercial launches, operational deployments or clinical effectiveness.

## Five concepts and primary users

| Project | Core concept reported by the article | Primary users reported by the article |
|---|---|---|
| **Kizuki Care** | AI-powered clinical documentation and care intelligence | Hospitals and healthcare providers |
| **Akira Care** | Knowledge-sharing platform for medical professionals | Healthcare institutions |
| **Kizuna Village** | Marketplace connecting corporations with rural communities | Businesses and local governments |
| **Anshin Care** | Smart medication adherence system | Older adults, caregivers and families |
| **Kizuna** | Robotics- and sensor-enabled elder-care safety network | Elder-care facilities |

**Kizuna Village and Kizuna are different projects.** The former connects corporations and rural communities; the latter addresses elder-care safety through robotics and sensors. No specific robot, sensor, medication-dispensing mechanism, software model or data integration is supplied. Do not infer such features from the short concept descriptions.

## How fieldwork shaped the portfolio

The article reports that CU Boulder and Nagoya University students transformed observations from Japan into ventures addressing shrinking societies. During the spring 2026 program, visits to startups, hospitals, research centers, rural communities and universities explored healthcare, mobility, caregiving and community resilience. Named contexts include Nagoya University Hospital's xR Center and rural healthcare in Nakatsugawa.

The author identifies shared themes across the projects: designing for entire systems, supporting clinicians, caregivers and communities, and grounding ideas in firsthand observation. AI is presented as a way to augment human capability. These are the author's educational interpretations, not evidence that the proposed systems were deployed or independently evaluated.

## Learning goals and limits

The article explicitly states that **not every concept is intended to become a company**. Entrepreneurial and intrapreneurial goals include identifying meaningful problems, testing assumptions, defining an addressable market, collaborating across cultures and communicating ideas clearly. Prototypes, pitches, professional portfolios and microcredentials are framed as evidence of learning.

The course architecture ends with final pitches at **Idea Stoa at NIC, Nagoya University**, followed by reflection and microcredentials. The reviewer corrected the article's erroneous “Idea Store” label on 2026-09-29. The article supplies no winning project, rankings, project ownership, individual team roster, incorporation details, customer traction or credential award conditions. A student's reference to competition results does not establish those missing facts.
