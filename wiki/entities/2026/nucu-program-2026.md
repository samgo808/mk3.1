# NUCU Build in Japan Program — 2026

The archived 2026 NUCU program, “NUCU 'Build in Japan' AI & Automation for Tomorrow's Societies,” connects Nagoya University (NU) and the University of Colorado Boulder (CU). Its program information describes BAIM 3300 as a three-credit CU course in which international student teams develop technology-driven startups addressing shrinking and aging populations. This page records the 2026 source description and does not establish current 2027 offerings or arrangements.

## Academic identity and credentials

The course is labeled “BAIM 3300 - AI & Automation for Tomorrow's Societies.” The source describes three academic credits at CU plus two micro-credentials: one through CU Innovation & Entrepreneurship and one through Ludus Labs. Its outline also describes certification from CU's Innovation & Entrepreneurship office and Ludus Labs in “Global Innovation & Entrepreneurship.” The document does not specify formal award titles, assessment requirements, or whether either credential was actually awarded.

## Format and timeline

The program outline advertises **15 days in Japan**, combining cultural exploration with business innovation. The promotional description begins with an online preparation course in **March**, followed by international teamwork at Nagoya University **“this May.”** The 2026 scope is assigned from the raw source's directory; exact dates and a daily itinerary are absent. These months must not be used as a 2027 schedule.

Students partner with Nagoya University students in cross-cultural cohort teams. The advertised activities include workshops, expert-led sessions, lectures, field visits, field trips, and visits to leading companies and innovation hubs. No companies or hubs are named in this source.

## Startup work and final pitch

Teams use AI, automation, advanced mobility, and other next-generation technologies to address aging and eldercare, labor shortages, urban-rural disparities, and mental health/social stability. Lean canvas planning, design thinking, primary market research, and global market testing support a complete business proposal from concept to execution. A Design Thinking Sprint includes prototyping.

The source describes a final pitch competition in Japan, with pitching at Nagoya University. It variously identifies business and healthcare entrepreneurship experts, academics, business leaders, and fellow student innovators as the panel or audience. It does not provide a named judging panel.

## Purpose and evidence limits

The program promotes international startup experience, AI and automation skills, exposure to global business and AI policy, leadership, cultural immersion, and professional networks. These are advertised benefits rather than verified outcomes. The source's unsupported prediction that over half of Japan's population will soon be over 65 is flagged in the source page and must not be treated as a fact.
