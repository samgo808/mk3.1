# Ludus Labs — 2026

In the website snapshot captured on **2026-08-24**, Ludus Labs describes itself as a startup incubator helping international university teams use emerging technologies to address healthcare, demographic change and education. Its guiding idea is that the best way to learn is to build. This archived 2026 account records how the organization presented itself, without establishing its current services or 2027 programs.

## Identity and purpose

The website uses “Build Globally” and “Building global impact through innovation.” It credits sponsors, university partners, volunteers and mentors, without naming individual supporters. It describes the founding group as educators, designers, entrepreneurs and researchers who give students tools, space and a little chaos to tackle urgent challenges. No individual founder, legal entity details or founding date are supplied.

According to the website, **Ludus** means both **play and school in Latin**. The name represents serious learning through playful experimentation. Ludus Labs says its role is to help students discover how to build, rather than tell them what to build, encouraging collaboration, creativity and courage that outlast a program.

## Japan and international collaboration

The organization says its work began in **Japan**, while its community is global. Programs such as **NUCU**, a collaboration between **Nagoya University (NU)** and the **University of Colorado (CU)**, bring together students across countries and disciplines in both **online and in-person** startup teams. The NUCU 2026 listing specifically names the University of Colorado, Boulder.

The snapshot promotes **15 days in Japan** through “NUCU 2026 - Build in Japan: AI & Automation for Tomorrow's Societies.” It describes global entrepreneurship, AI-driven automation, cross-cultural collaboration, a design sprint, test market research and an international pitch competition. The phrase “Study Abroad Meets a Startup Studio” summarizes the advertised format. See [[nucu-program-2026]] for separately sourced credits and itinerary details.

## Participants and impact areas

Four intended audiences are **Purpose Driven Builders**, **Creative Technologists**, **Systems Hackers**, and **Global Team Players**. They represent students creating meaningful ventures, designers of immersive human-centered technology experiences, experimenters tackling complex problems, and collaborators working across cultures and time zones. These descriptions do not establish admission requirements.

The example challenges are healthcare for societies living longer, turning demographic change into opportunities for innovation, and building the future of learning. The organization presents technology as a tool for impact and teamwork as part of learning.

## Learning and alumni community

Advertised benefits include cross-cultural communication, business-model validation, applied AI knowledge, and professional networks with innovators, entrepreneurs and business professionals. The website offers a **digital certificate**, with academic credit **at eligible schools**. It does not specify credential award conditions or guarantee academic credit for every participant.

The website also advertises **lifetime alumni-community access** and a **chance to return as an ambassador** to help lead future programs. These remain source promises, not independently verified entitlements or guaranteed appointments. See [[nucu-startup-learning-2026]].
