# NUCU Healthcare and Mobility Sites — 2026

The archived 2026 NUCU Program Guide connects **Nagoya University Hospital's xR Center**, **NCGG**, **Professor Hirofumi Aoki's mobility research** and clinical-innovation sessions with the needs of aging societies. These are the guide's educational descriptions and planned visits, not verified present-day institutional profiles. Source pages 13, 18–24 describe user needs, safety, evidence and implementation alongside technology.

## Nagoya University Hospital xR Center

**xR** means extended reality, collectively including **virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR)**. The guide links these technologies to simulation-based learning, surgical education, patient communication and interdisciplinary collaboration. It describes anatomy visualization, clinical scenarios, procedural training and understanding complex medical conditions.

The educational rationale is to practice clinical decisions, procedures, communication and teamwork without putting patients at risk. Immersive learning may also support training across locations with limited access to clinical settings. The guide positions the center as a meeting point of medicine, clinical practice, engineering and education, complementing startup/business-development visits.

The entrepreneurial lesson is to understand patients, professionals, educators and institutions before designing tools, then evaluate practical, ethical and social value. The May 18 itinerary identifies Dr Nakanishi–GI and Ms. Sato for this session, with an earlier hospital tour by Dr Sumigama–OBGY.

## National Center for Geriatrics and Gerontology (NCGG)

The guide describes NCGG as a national research and medical center combining clinical care, research, rehabilitation and social implementation for older adults' physical/mental health and lifelong independence. Named needs include **dementia care, frailty prevention, mobility, rehabilitation, social participation and independent living**.

The **Assistive Robot Center** connects technological “seeds” to actual elderly-care needs, offers testing fields from a longevity-medicine perspective, promotes rehabilitation-engineering research and contributes policy recommendations. Students were to study development, evaluation and use of assistive devices and care robots, asking how they support dignity, independence, safety, mobility and quality of life.

The guide contrasts startup business models/platforms/markets with clinical evidence, user-centered design, safety evaluation and long-term care needs. Successful ideas should serve caregivers, professionals, older adults and communities, rather than equate technical novelty with usefulness. The visit was scheduled May 20, followed by lunch at Agri Town Genki.

## Professor Hirofumi Aoki and mobility

The guide associates Professor Hirofumi Aoki at Nagoya University with future mobility, **driving simulation, autonomous driving, smart cities and human-centered mobility systems**. It describes work bridging engineering, medicine and public policy, collaborations with governments, companies and research institutes, autonomous-shuttle trials and social implementation, and supervision of student transportation/health projects. These roles are source descriptions, not a current employment verification.

His NIC driving-simulation session was planned for May 20 at 14:00. The learning connection is transportation barriers in aging urban and rural communities and integration of mobility systems with health needs.

## Medical innovation sessions

**Professor Masakatsu Fukai:** the guide calls him a former Designated Professor in Nagoya University's Medical and Healthcare Innovation Unit, Academic Research & Industry-Academia-Government Collaboration. It traces a career from **Bank of Tokyo to Nidec Corporation**, then Nagoya University in **2013**. His session connects clinical needs, university research, companies, technology, startups and business development. It describes **biodesign** as identifying healthcare problems before developing solutions.

**Dr. Shintaro Oyama:** the session concerns medical technology, clinical needs and innovation. Students were to consider real clinical problems as starting points, combining medical knowledge, technology and user-centered thinking amid rising demand, limited resources and diverse older-adult needs.
