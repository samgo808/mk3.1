# NUCU Session 3: Postwar Growth, Lost Decades and AI Demonstrations — 2026-01-27

The archived January 27, 2026 BAIM 3300 lecture, “Reinvention After Collapse: Economic Reconstruction Japan from Shōwa to Heisei (Post-war): A Case Study,” covers postwar growth, the asset bubble and stagnation, then demonstrates custom AI assistants. It also records course logistics as stated that evening. Historical, financial and product claims are preserved as lecture statements, not current advice or independently verified facts.

## Reconstruction and institutions

Samuel Goodman frames postwar growth as **1945–1989**, followed by lost decades. World War II itself is intentionally skipped; an opening territorial-map description is incomplete and must not be reconstructed from unseen slides. The lecture cites Japan at **53% of prewar size**, without a clear metric, and Tokyo falling from **4.5 million to 2.5 million**. These are ambiguous source figures, not established population/economic statistics.

The speaker begins the reconstruction account at **1946**. The strategic pivot is from strong armies to strong companies. **MITI (Ministry of International Trade and Industry)** coordinates steel, shipbuilding, electronics and autos, loans and foreign-technology licensing while limiting foreign competitors. **FILP (Fiscal Investment and Loan Program)** channels household/postal savings into loans for industrial development; “one savings account” is a simplified description.

**Akio Morita** (transcribed “Akil/Merida”), born in the Nagoya region to a sake-producing family, is the visionary Sony example. The lecture says he met his cofounder during the war and declined teaching careers. It describes rejecting a **100,000-unit Bulova** order that required replacing Sony's brand, and a prediction that Sony would be as famous in **50 years**. The transcript calls the product watches/electronics inconsistently and compares the order with company market capitalization; preserve the branding lesson without asserting those details as verified. **Honda** is another visionary example.

The **Walkman** demonstrates portable personal music and craftsmanship; the lecture gives **50 million units**, **1983**, and Oxford Dictionary recognition in a compressed passage whose relationship is unclear. A commercial is played; the instructor recalls the later CD version. Sony radios and TVs extend the example.

## Employment, growth and collapse

The **salaryman** is described through loyalty, long hours, unpaid overtime and a reciprocal lifetime-employment expectation, plus after-work socializing. This is a broad historical model, not an employment guarantee for all Japanese workers. The lecture cites average **10% annual growth**, GDP from **$24 billion in 1955** to **$1 trillion in the 1980s**, and a number-two economy behind the US. It claims **32 of the top 50 corporations in 1988** were Japanese versus only **Toyota** at lecture time; ranking basis/date is not supplied.

The bubble mechanism is land appreciation → collateral → borrowing → stocks/more land → more appreciation. Memorable comparisons say the **Imperial Palace grounds** exceeded California's value and a Tokyo parking lot exceeded Central Park's. Its palace area of **300 square yards** is implausible and unverified; a comparison with **CU South Campus beside US 36 toward Denver** is an anecdotal size analogy, not a measured area. The lecture describes easy **100-million-yen** loans, loosely converted as **$2 million then / half a million dollars now**; these are not reliable exchange-rate guidance.

Policy tightening is described as **five rate increases in less than/about a year, 3.25% to 6%**, loan caps and tax changes. Claimed consequences include **$10 trillion** erased in a little over a year, **17,000 Sony jobs** cut in the late 1990s, **68%** more bankruptcies and **10,000 companies** failing. Exact bases and periods are not documented. The broken employment promise undermines trust.

**Zombie companies** survive continued lending because banks resist recognizing losses; resources are trapped instead of moving toward innovation. The **lost decade**, roughly **1991–2001**, has a quoted **1.14% growth rate** with metric unspecified, and the lecturer notes some extend the stagnation much longer. Garbled sentences about labor/capital are not reconstructed.

## Innovation and the Galapagos analogy

**Galapagos syndrome** compares locally optimized technology with species thriving in a contained environment. **i-mode** offers email, QR codes, cashless payments and TV about **five years before iPhone**, but local systems constrain global adoption. **Sharp/Foxconn** and **Toshiba's seven-year profit-inflation scandal** illustrate decline. These simplified company histories are not independently checked.

**Monozukuri** means dedication to craftsmanship in this account; perfection and consensus can delay releases compared with agile/fail-fast approaches. Sony Blu-ray versus Netflix streaming, and combustion-engine optimization versus Tesla's “computers on wheels,” illustrate business-model change. Japan's **125-million** domestic market is contrasted with South Korea's **51-million** market, allegedly pushing **Samsung/LG** abroad. These are lecture-time rounded comparisons; demographic decline challenges reliance on domestic scale.

## Discussion and further reading

Students are regrouped by **vanilla, chocolate or strawberry ice-cream preference**, explicitly a mixing exercise, not validated personality science. Questions weigh stable employment for 125 million people against risky global expansion, and lifetime loyalty against US job-hopping/salary freedom. Discussions consider risk aversion, leadership, mobility and protection from AI displacement. The instructor notes April school/corporate recruiting seasons, year-group hiring and salaryman suits; fax machines, dedicated **IC transit cards** and niche vending machines are observation prompts for alternative innovation standards.

The analogy with Edo isolation is qualified: modern barriers may be organizational consensus, not closed foreign trade. A WWII question raises colonial atrocities and memorial visits; the instructor offers an empire/status interpretation while explicitly lacking an answer for ongoing denial/commemoration. It is not an excuse for atrocities or an established consensus account. References include an academic **MITI/Japanese miracle** work (full title absent), **Lost Japan by Alex Kerr** (infrastructure/cemented rivers anecdote), and **Dan Carlin's Supernova in the East**, described as six/seven parts; Genghis Khan podcasts are also mentioned. Institutional/biographical claims about speakers and resources remain unverified.

## Demonstrated AI assistants

A **Charlie Munger Investment Assistant** combines a persona prompt, interview/profile PDFs, suggested questions and an AI-written **JavaScript API action** for stock quotes; an **Apple** price lookup is demonstrated without a recoverable numeric price here. It illustrates **instructions + knowledge + tools**, not approved financial advice. The lecturer notes such custom work can become redundant as base products add capabilities.

First-generation **Makoto-kun** uses uploaded lecture/meeting/schedule documents in a custom GPT, with a growing prompt and perceived slowness. A “5/20/25”/Station Ai itinerary lookup refers to **last year's schedule**, not a 2026 trip date. The **n8n** version (transcribed “NAN”) shows selectable models, a text brochure, possible email escalation and coffee-chat scheduling. A later **2.3 RAG** version is planned to retrieve relevant chunks instead of reading the whole document. These are archived demonstrations/plans, not current interface or permission policy.

**Weela AI** is a personal example using the lecturer's mother's **StoryWorth** answers: weekly emailed life questions accumulated as knowledge for a persona with Spanish/English conversation. He clarifies she is alive. It motivates loneliness, memory preservation and questions about simulation versus a person. No private life-answer content is compiled; the human reviewer approved public release of the named family-related example and unpublished prototypes on 2026-09-30. See [[nucu-ai-assistant-demonstrations-2026]].
