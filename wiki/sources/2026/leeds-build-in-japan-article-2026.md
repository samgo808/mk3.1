# Leeds Build in Japan Article — 2026

The archived Leeds article “Designing Tomorrow’s Societies: How Leeds Students Created Real-World Solutions in Japan” reports that **20 Leeds students** participated in spring **2026**, working with Nagoya University peers in partnership with Ludus Labs. It describes a people-first BAIM 3300 course, field research, five venture concepts and student reflections. These are the article's retrospective reports, not independently verified outcomes or 2027 arrangements.

## Course, partners and participation

**AI & Automation for Tomorrow’s Societies (BAIM 3300)** is offered through the **Management Information Systems program**. Its “Build in Japan” initiative brings CU Boulder and Nagoya University students together for field research, cross-cultural collaboration and venture concepts addressing real human needs. The article describes Ludus Labs as an **innovation education company** connecting students, universities and industry through technology, entrepreneurship and international experiential learning.

The account reports **20 Leeds students**, **two weeks in Tokyo and Nagoya**, and later a **15-day experience**. Both duration descriptions are preserved without treating rounded wording as a separate itinerary. Startups, hospitals, research centers, rural communities and universities supplied learning about healthcare, mobility, caregiving and community resilience.

## Course architecture

The seven stages appear in this order:

1. **Foundation:** Japan's historical, institutional and demographic transformation.
2. **Technology Primer:** TCP/IP, OSI, workflow systems, automation, AI, LLMs and AI agents.
3. **Design Sprint:** ideation, product sketches and venture development.
4. **Global Collaboration:** CU Boulder + Nagoya University teams.
5. **Field Validation:** hospitals, startups, robotics labs, elder-care facilities and rural communities.
6. **Venture Presentation:** final pitches at **Idea Stoa at NIC, Nagoya University**, per the reviewer correction recorded above.
7. **Reflection & Microcredentials:** portable credentials, professional portfolios and future learning.

The prose also names AI models, **SaaS platforms**, prototypes and stakeholder validation. It asks how technology should serve societies facing urgent challenges and contrasts “What can AI build?” with **“What should AI build?”** See [[nucu-course-architecture-2026]].

## Fieldwork and human-centered innovation

Japan's aging population is presented alongside labor shortages, healthcare demands, rural depopulation and social isolation. Named visits include **Google for Startups**, **Techstars Tokyo**, **Nagoya University Hospital**, its **xR Center**, and rural healthcare facilities in **Nakatsugawa**. Students engaged with founders, clinicians, researchers, caregivers and public-sector leaders.

The article glosses **xR** as **AR/VR/MR** and connects immersive technologies with medical training, patient communication and clinical teamwork. Nakatsugawa visits illustrated effects of population decline on healthcare access, local economies and community life. The principle is that AI should **augment human capability**, with ethics and human needs guiding technology choices.

## Student venture portfolio

| Project | Core concept | Primary users |
|---|---|---|
| Kizuki Care | AI-powered clinical documentation and care intelligence | Hospitals and healthcare providers |
| Akira Care | Knowledge-sharing platform for medical professionals | Healthcare institutions |
| Kizuna Village | Marketplace connecting corporations with rural communities | Businesses and local governments |
| Anshin Care | Smart medication adherence system | Older adults, caregivers and families |
| Kizuna | Robotics- and sensor-enabled elder-care safety network | Elder-care facilities |

**Kizuna** and **Kizuna Village** are separate concepts. The article says not every concept is intended to become a company. It emphasizes system-wide solutions supporting clinicians, caregivers and communities, and entrepreneurial/intrapreneurial skills: identifying problems, testing assumptions, defining an addressable market, working across cultures and communicating clearly. No launches, deployments, clinical results or individual team membership are established. See [[nucu-student-venture-portfolio-2026]].

## Career evidence and anonymous reflections

The article presents microcredentials and venture portfolios as evidence of **global entrepreneurship, design thinking, AI and automation, primary market research, venture pitching and cross-cultural collaboration**. It provides no award conditions or individual credential records. Its closing argument joins technological fluency with empathy, curiosity and responsible decisions, combining cultural learning, AI and entrepreneurship.

One anonymous participant praises Professor Brady's lifelong-learning approach, tools for learning emerging technology and ethical emphasis. Four themed reflections concern **the resilience of Japanese elderly people and care workers**, **presentation-driven personal growth**, **creating a usable prototype and presenting before a board**, and **learning from another culture's thinking**. These are participant perspectives, not measured outcomes.
