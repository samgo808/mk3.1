# NUCU Program Information — 2026

The archived 2026 NUCU program information describes “NUCU 'Build in Japan' AI & Automation for Tomorrow's Societies,” a Nagoya University–University of Colorado Boulder initiative. The source presents BAIM 3300 as a three-credit experiential course combining international startup teamwork, AI and automation, and a 15-day Japan experience. ## Program, credits, and activities

Nagoya University (NU) and the University of Colorado Boulder (CU) bring students together in international cohort teams to design and launch technology-driven ventures addressing a shrinking global population. BAIM 3300 carries three CU academic credits. The source describes two micro-credentials: one through CU Innovation & Entrepreneurship and one through Ludus Labs. The outline calls the certification “Global Innovation & Entrepreneurship” and identifies CU's Innovation & Entrepreneurship office; formal credential titles and award conditions are not specified.

The outline advertises 15 days in Japan exploring culture and business innovation. Its “Top 5 Reasons” section places an online preparation course in March and intercultural teamwork at Nagoya University “this May.” Those relative months belong to the archived 2026 source context, not a current schedule.

Students use lean canvas planning, design thinking, primary market research, lean startup tools, and global market testing to develop a complete business proposal from concept to execution. A Design Thinking Sprint includes prototyping. Activities include field visits, workshops, expert-led sessions, lectures, field trips, and visits to leading companies and innovation hubs. Topics specifically include AI & Ethics, AI & Epistemology, and How to Know Truth.

The program culminates in a final pitch competition in Japan. The overview describes a panel of business and healthcare entrepreneurship experts; the outline describes pitching at Nagoya University before academics, business leaders, and fellow student innovators. These are complementary descriptions, not a confirmed roster.

## Technologies, challenges, and Japan rationale

The proposed ventures use AI, automation, advanced mobility, and other next-generation technologies. The four named challenge areas are:

1. Aging populations and advanced elder care technology.
2. Labor shortages and workforce automation.
3. Urban-rural disparities and smart infrastructure adaptation.
4. Mental health and digital tools for social stability.

The source presents Japan as a testing ground for AI and robotics to improve quality of life and health, citing demographic aging, technological innovation, advanced infrastructure, and openness to digital transformation. It connects eastern and western societal challenges and proposes applying insights from Japan elsewhere.

The five advertised reasons to join are: experience a “Living Lab of the Future”; “Collaborate Globally, Innovate Locally”; engage with experts and bold ideas; launch a future career; and transformative growth. Claimed benefits include cross-cultural networks, cultural immersion, peer and mentor connections, real-world startup experience, exposure to AI-driven economies and global business/policy, innovation and automation skills, critical thinking, adaptability, purposeful innovation, and leadership.

## Anonymous student testimony and advice

The source contains 11 testimonial passages. Their distinct points, paraphrased here, are:

1. Challenge oneself through international teamwork on real-world problems.
2. Learn to work without prescribed guidelines, with collaboration, intercultural understanding, and flexibility.
3. Gain knowledge and hands-on experience that stimulate curiosity about innovation, culture, and perspectives.
4. Gain new perspectives through site visits and meeting people.
5. Use program connections later in life.
6. Apply market-survey skills to a career.
7. Build connections and work better with international colleagues.
8. Observe shrinking/aging societies firsthand, brainstorm solutions, and transfer innovation and entrepreneurship skills to one's own country.
9. Discover healthcare as an interest; connect entrepreneurship and strategy with process improvement, communication, design, leadership, and open-ended problems.
10. Apply fieldwork, problem solving, and business logistics to future work and aging challenges in other countries or towns.
11. Develop interest in global entrepreneurship and its worldwide innovation potential.

Four advice passages recommend understanding people's pain points before choosing a specific, innovative idea; asking many questions about Japanese healthcare and advancement while developing teamwork and emotional intelligence; bringing authenticity and expecting personal/professional growth; and leaving one's comfort zone to try different tools.
