# NUCU Session 6: Reiwa, Shrinking Society and Makoto-kun — 2026-02-17

The archived February 17, 2026 BAIM 3300 lecture, “LLMs: Reinvention Without a Reset Button: Societal Redesign, Japan in the Reiwa Era: A Case Study / Tech Demo (Makoto-kun intro),” connects population aging with institutions, entrepreneurship and technology. Its design challenge is to help society adapt to demographic change and **shrink gracefully**, rather than promise to reverse population decline. Numerical and policy claims below are attributed to this lecture, not independently verified statistics or current advice.

## Reiwa and the demographic argument

Japan uses both the Western calendar and imperial eras. The lecture identifies **Reiwa 8** in 2026, glosses Reiwa as “beautiful harmony,” and illustrates town-office forms requiring a birth year expressed in **Showa**. A personal birth-year detail is excluded from this teaching account.

An unnamed town outside Kyoto reportedly has **fewer than 50 people**. A resident makes puppets to replace absent neighbors; a possible **60 Minutes** report is mentioned without a citation. Later the lecture says the town had its **first birth in 20 years**. The town is not identified, and these details must not be attached to a guessed location.

The lecture links postwar growth in **1960–1980** to baby boomers and lifetime salaryman employment, then the 1980s bubble and lost decades to prolonged stagnation. It cites **3.3** fertility in roughly the 1960s, **2.1** as replacement level, and approximately **1.2** for contemporary Japan, **1.6** for the US and **0.7** for South Korea. Fertility is discussed as children per woman/couple in simplified language, not a percentage, despite some transcript percent signs. The exact reference years and statistical series are missing.

Other lecture figures include **730,000 births / 1.58 million deaths in 2024**, and population shrinking by **1.7 people per minute**. Those figures do not reconcile directly; the speaker also conflates death rate with net population loss. Preserve them as inconsistent source claims, not one calculated estimate. A wage slide is described as about **$38,000 in 2015** and **$40,000 in 2024**, with real wages and living costs relatively flat for about **30 years**, then recent inflation. The dollar conversion, wage definition and underlying chart are unavailable.

The population-pyramid comparison moves from many young people supporting a small older cohort in the **1950s** toward an inverted triangle. For the **2025 problem**, the lecture says baby boomers turned **75**, giving an estimated **four workers per retiree in 1990** versus **1.2** now. It describes retirement ages **60 / 65** and possible **70 / 75** changes, but does not establish statutory rules. Nearly **30%** over **65**, a **50%** Self-Defense Force recruiting shortfall, and Japan's move from the second to **fourth-largest economy**, behind Germany and China as well as the US, are further dated claims.

## Marriage, work, gender and solitude

The lecture estimates only **2%** of births occur outside marriage, so declining marriage affects fertility. It describes **omiai** (transcribed “omiyai,” arranged introductions/marriage), common through the early **1980s** in its account, and workplace marriage under lifetime employment as weakened institutions. This is a broad interpretation, not evidence that arranged marriage disappeared completely.

Women reportedly make up **45%** of the workforce while still bearing disproportionate housework and childcare. Child-rearing is said to consume **50%** of a typical couple's income in Japan versus **30%** in the US; no method or denominator is provided. Long hours and staying until the boss leaves reduce family time even after lifetime employment weakens. **Karoshi**, death by overwork, is illustrated with the public **NHK** case named **Miwa Sato**, dated **2013**, with roughly **160 overtime hours**. The name and case details need verification; this is not a private student's health record.

**Ohitorisama / hitori-sama** describes solo lifestyles: dining, karaoke and weddings. The public holographic-partner example is named **Akihito Kondo** in the transcript; spelling is unverified. The instructor distinguishes a quiet ramen booth designed for concentrating on taste and smell from a solo barbecue grill replacing traditionally social dining. Technology may support people but also substitute for relationships.

## Silver-economy examples and local decline

- **Unicharm:** the lecture says adult diaper production exceeds baby diapers, illustrating a changed customer base.
- **Ito En / Itoen:** thicker, gelatinous tea is described as a product for seniors with swallowing difficulties. This is a market example, not a medical recommendation.
- **Houseboat Club:** funeral services scattering ashes in Tokyo Bay/Harbor, reportedly **28 people** on a boat, illustrate needs of people without family support.
- **Kodokushi**, solitary death, appears in the source topic list and an anonymous property-management anecdote. No individual's private medical or housing record is reproduced.
- **450–500 school closures annually**, **896 municipalities facing extinction by 2040**, and a school closing after its last **two students** graduate illustrate rural decline. The underlying reports are not supplied.
- Cheap **ghost homes** do not solve the lack of jobs, peers or children. Teachers cover clubs and tutoring; outsourcing to private firms still fails if too few children remain. **Cram schools**, university entrance exams described as occurring in the junior year of high school, and the link between university prestige and company recruitment illustrate education pressure. The exam timing and generalizations are unverified. AI tutoring is suggested as an opportunity, not a demonstrated replacement.

Government responses in the lecture include rural relocation cash incentives and a **Tokyo dating app** with strict proof-of-single-status requirements. The instructor argues that time and work-life balance can be more fundamental than matching partners. The immigration discussion gives inconsistent **less than 5% / less than 3% foreign population**, **98% homogeneity**, and an alleged government **10%** target, without sources or compatible definitions. A volunteer cafe involving Indian IT workers is an integration example; Japanese-language barriers and convenience-store employment are observations.

An unnamed study is summarized as immigration slowing rather than reversing decline because immigrants also age; the transcript's wording here is garbled. The claim about children being sent to home countries is unsupported. These claims do not establish that immigration has one inevitable demographic effect.

## Shrinking gracefully and classroom questions

The instructor emphasizes that stagnation has not meant societal collapse: social cohesion, healthcare and a competitive economy remain assets. **Kintsugi**, repairing pottery with lacquer dusted with gold or silver, makes breakage part of the object's history rather than hiding it. It is a metaphor for adaptation, not a literal economic prescription.

Discussion asks whether population decline is **an economic failure or a market correction toward a sustainable society**, and whether the US shift from dating to swiping resembles Japan's weakened family-formation institutions. Arguments include workplace structures making family life unsustainable; economic effects following social change rather than necessarily causing it; and success depending on whether a changing market serves a smaller population well. Others argue dating apps save time and money, or distinguish arranged-marriage pressure from lower-commitment swiping. No consensus is established.

Classroom comments question robotic elder care that removes human company, while accepting help with chores, transport or other tasks. **Tesla Optimus**, robot bartenders and autonomous hospital transport arise as discussion examples, not verified deployment claims. A robot-bartender exchange contrasts handling one bottle with a human handling two and mentions **$30,000** without a product or reliable price source. A separate garbled **80%** remark has no recoverable denominator and is not converted into a statistic. The field-observation prompt is: where does technology replace labor **out of necessity**, not just efficiency? A robot in **STATION Ai** and a **Tokyo Skytree** booking requiring fax confirmation illustrate coexisting advanced and dated technology; the robot's precise job is explicitly unknown.

## A third reinvention, startup finance and Society 5.0

The lecture recaps Meiji transformation after **Perry's black ships**, postwar economic reinvention, then asks whether Reiwa can produce a third transformation. It cites a **2022** initiative of **10 trillion yen / $67 billion**, with goals of **100 unicorns** and **100,000 startups** “by next year” (relative to this 2026 lecture). A unicorn is defined as a startup valued at **$1 billion**. The speaker reports only **seven** unicorns and about **$5 billion VC investment in 2024**. The transcript does not distinguish spending already made from an investment target; the numbers remain unverified.

US exits are described as approximately **10% IPO**, versus Japan's **70% IPO / 30% acquisition**. A **$100 million valuation/market cap** is described as enough for a Japanese “mini IPO,” comparable to a US **Series B**. These are classroom comparisons, not exchange listing rules. The institutional question is whether early listing and salaryman risk aversion encourage compliance and stagnation rather than growth.

**Society 5.0** is presented as connecting cyberspace and the physical world through **AI, IoT and robotics** to address aging, labor shortage and rural depopulation. The sequence is **1.0 hunter-gatherer → 2.0 agricultural → 3.0 industrial → 4.0 information → 5.0 cyber-physical society**. Investment alone is not presented as sufficient.

Q&A raises foreign property purchases, speculation, skiing/telecommuting, digital-nomad and startup visas, and possible changes under the administration referred to as “Sanae-san.” Visa categories are blurred and no eligibility rules are established. The answer about government ownership of foreign-founded startups is tentative, with defense technology as a possible exception. A claim that **Notion's founders built the company in Tokyo while US-registered** is an unverified anecdote.

## Historical Makoto-kun demonstration

**Makoto-kun 1.0** used a custom GPT; **2.0** used **n8n**, transcribed “NAN,” with brochure text. The brochure consumed roughly **2,800 tokens** when included whole. **2.3** used **RAG, retrieval-augmented generation**, to retrieve chunks from a database rather than inject every document. Program information and the **January 20 session 2 transcript** were demonstrated as GitHub-hosted inputs. The instructor says pre/post-class chatter had been removed before that older upload; this is not permission to alter today's immutable raw files.

Demo questions include why to join NUCU, a January 20 lecture summary, and historical events such as sakoku and black ships. **Discord** supplied an interface. Email escalation and coffee-chat scheduling were described, but the calendar was **separate and still a test**, and a general-channel reply **failed live**. These observations do not verify present MK3.1 tools or deployment.

The archived Discord onboarding described a **seven-day invitation**, a verification room with **three introduction questions** (their exact text is absent), a server nickname of first name/last initial/school, and a green verification mark before other channels opened. Public-channel conversations were visible to other members; DMs were also offered. The human reviewer approved publication of these internal onboarding details on 2026-09-30; no invitation, private URL or credential is included.
