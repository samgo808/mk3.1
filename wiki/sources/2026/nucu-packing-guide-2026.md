# NUCU Packing Guide — 2026

The archived 2026 NUCU Packing Guide recommends packing light, doing laundry during the trip and keeping essential documents and small daily-use items in a backpack. It also supplies a Japanese phrase sheet and informal etiquette advice. Its 2026 scope comes from the source directory; the text supplies no publication date, named hotel or airline-specific baggage allowance, and does not establish 2027 requirements.

## Pack-light strategy and wardrobe

The goal is **one carry-on-sized or medium checked-bag-sized rolling suitcase plus one backpack**. The recommended medium suitcase is approximately **60–65 cm tall, 25–28 cm deep, 40–45 cm wide, or 60–75 liters**. The guide says this size is manageable and fits Shinkansen overhead racks; that assertion is not a verified universal luggage rule or an airline carry-on allowance.

The base wardrobe uses layers:

- **1 suit / dress or suit jacket** for presentation day and some site visits; the source lists alternatives without further dress-code detail.
- **2–3 collared shirts** for site visits.
- **2 pairs of pants**, with lightweight chinos/cotton suggested for faster drying in hotel laundry.
- **3–4 tops**, mixing T-shirts and one long-sleeve.
- **1 lightweight jacket/windbreaker**, ideally water-resistant for spring showers.
- **5 sets of socks and underwear**, with washing halfway through the trip. High-quality socks without holes are emphasized because shoes will be removed frequently.

## Shoes, rain and phone power

Comfortable walking shoes should be broken in before departure. The guide estimates **15,000–20,000 steps a day**, not a guaranteed daily requirement. Slip-on shoes are suggested for temples and certain restaurants, and flip-flops for moving around the hotel.

Bring a small umbrella or poncho. The guide predicts rain at least once and says a functional folding umbrella can be bought for **under $10** at a convenience store; these are source expectations, not verified weather or current prices. A **portable power bank** supports heavy Google Maps and translation-app use.

## Laundry and items to avoid overpacking

The guide says “our hotel” will have coin laundry, but does not identify the property. It describes many machines as combined **Wash & Dry** units and advises checking the machine. It says most dispense detergent automatically; otherwise, buy **one-load packets at the hotel or a convenience store (Konbini)**. Small evening batches during social time avoid waiting late for drying.

Avoid excessive “just in case” outfits; **Uniqlo and GU** are named for affordable basics. Avoid a different shoe pair for each outfit: the suggested limit is one worn pair and possibly nicer sneakers in the bag. The guide also discourages **bulky towels**, saying hotels provide them, and **full-sized toiletries**, suggesting travel sizes or hotel shampoo/soap. These hotel assumptions must not be extended to every accommodation.

## Personal-item essentials

- **Passport and travel-insurance documents:** secure internal pocket; take photos or photocopies as backups.
- **Hand towel / tenugui / handkerchief:** the guide says public restrooms often lack paper towels or hand dryers and describes locals carrying a small towel.
- **Coin purse:** useful for **100- and 500-yen coins**; “cash-heavy” is the guide's broad description of Japan.
- **Ziplock or small plastic trash bag:** hold rubbish where bins are sparse.
- **American candy or snacks:** suggested for interactions with Nagoya students.

## Language and etiquette

The “Essential 10” lists **Konnichiwa; Sumimasen; Onegaishimasu; Hai / Iie; Wakarimasen; Eigo o hanasemasu ka?; Ikura desu ka?; ___ wa doko desu ka?; Oishikatta desu!** It contains nine grouped entries, with Yes/No paired; no missing tenth entry is invented. Exact meanings, usage examples, vowel mnemonics and the **approximately 15-degree bow** suggestion are preserved in [[nucu-japanese-language-basics-2026]]. The guide encourages quieter conversation on commuter trains and values communicative effort over perfect grammar.

## Conflicting information and scope limits

The Packing Guide's hotel-towel/toiletry and automatic-detergent claims do not name Yamate South. Preserve this accommodation distinction; the hotel advice does not supersede the residence-specific requirements. This comparison uses existing wiki evidence, not a new ingestion of the Program Guide.

The guide recommends flip-flops/slip-ons, while also suggesting a minimal shoe count; these are flexible recommendations rather than a single rigid checklist.
