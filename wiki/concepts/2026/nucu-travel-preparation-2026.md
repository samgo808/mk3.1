# NUCU Travel Preparation and Daily Living — 2026

The archived NUCU 2026 Program Guide describes travel preparation, personal supplies, insurance, meals, payments and connectivity for the May 2026 trip; the Packing Guide adds a pack-light strategy and language advice. Prices, availability, banking claims and food/health guidance below are source statements, not verified current advice or 2027 requirements. Existing Program Guide coverage comes from pages 1, 14–15 and 42–46; the separately attributed Packing Guide additions were approved for public publication on 2026-09-28.

## Packing and before departure

The list names **passport; thermometer; clothing for the stay; masks for at least five days; Japanese cash equivalent to approximately US $200; prescription medicine; short-trip backpack; towels; laptop/tablet; phone with translation app; travel-insurance emergency contacts; rain gear; a light jacket for air conditioning; and converter if needed**. Prohibited imports must not be brought. The source says underlined items are mandatory, but underlining is absent from the text export: do not infer a complete mandatory list. The thermometer is explicitly required in the prose.

The guide describes rainy season as early June to mid-July and increasing rain toward late May. It says a nearby shopping mall has **UNIQLO, DAISO, a pharmacy, groceries, restaurants, Burger King and Starbucks**, without naming its location. Missing nonmandatory supplies can be bought locally. Yamate South supplies no towels, shampoo or detergent; laundry is paid.

Students should recheck e-tickets, arrive early and ask airlines about baggage, excess weight and meals. The guide says most airlines allow online check-in **24 hours** before departure and suggests putting an email in the reservation for schedule/cancellation alerts. No airline-specific guarantee is given.

## Packing Guide additions and accommodation differences

The **2026 Packing Guide** recommends one rolling suitcase plus one backpack, a layered wardrobe, laundry halfway through the trip, broken-in walking shoes, a power bank and rain protection. Its exact clothing quantities, suggested suitcase dimensions and **15,000–20,000 daily-step estimate** are preserved in [[nucu-packing-guide-2026]]. It adds passport/insurance-document copies, a hand towel, coin purse, small trash bag and American snacks for meeting Nagoya students. See [[nucu-japanese-language-basics-2026]] for its phrase sheet.

### Conflicting information — hotel advice versus Yamate South

The Packing Guide says hotels provide towels and shampoo/soap and most laundry machines dispense detergent. The Program Guide's residence-specific account says **Yamate South provides no towels, shampoo or laundry detergent**. The Packing Guide names no hotel or machine, so its advice must not override the Yamate South requirements. A compact hand towel for public restrooms is also distinct from a bathing towel. These statements may describe different accommodations; the reviewer chose to keep this scope difference explicit.

## Insurance and emergencies

**Insurance is not included in the program fee.** The guide requires comprehensive international travel insurance purchased **before leaving home**, covering illness, hospitalization and repatriation across the outbound flight, stay and return home. Carry insurer emergency contact details.

The source gives **119 for an ambulance** and **110 for police**, asks participants to cooperate, and says to contact **Dr. Hitoki Hasegawa** as soon as possible during such emergencies. He is also the travel-problem contact. Personal contact values are retained in the source page's contact record as explicitly instructed by the reviewer on 2026-09-28; page-level public-release approval was granted on 2026-09-28.

## Meals and allergies

Nagoya University provides some meals; participants cover others. The guide describes Tsurumai's cafeteria as **cash-only, approximately ¥600 per meal** (some cheaper menus); other restaurants as accepting cards, **¥1,000–¥1,500 per meal**; and convenience stores as accepting cards. It estimates **about ¥20,000 for one week's meals**, with individual variation, and calls fruit/vegetables comparatively expensive. These estimates are not reconciled into a guaranteed budget.

Vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and halal choices are described as limited. Convenience stores near the residence offer lunch boxes. Check ingredients when uncertain. The guide's seven-item allergen list is **shrimp (えび), crab (かに), wheat (こむぎ), buckwheat (そば), egg (たまご), milk (ぎゅうにゅう), peanuts (らっかせい)**. It is not an independently verified current or exhaustive allergen-labeling list.

## Money, power and connectivity

The guide mentions international **VISA, MasterCard, Plus and Cirrus** ATM access at some banks/post offices, Sakae/Nagoya stations, department stores and hotels, with hours not always 24/7. Confirm card eligibility before travel. It mentions **Alipay and UnionPay**, exchange of US dollars, UK pounds, Chinese yuan, Korean won and Australian dollars, and airport banks/post offices or a campus post office until **16:00 weekdays**. Its claim about **Citibank branches at Chubu International Airport and Sakae** is unverified and must not be used as a current service guarantee.

Nagoya power is described as **100V, AC60Hz, Type A plugs**. The guide asks participants to confirm home-university **eduroam** login before arrival; it describes free campus/accommodation access, but does not guarantee access without eligibility. Reliable internet is needed for a **short daily feedback form**. Arrange an alternative when eduroam is unavailable and consider portable Wi-Fi off campus; public Wi-Fi may not meet every need.

No general university computer labs are offered in the guide. Bring a laptop/tablet; convenience-store copy machines are suggested for printing, with a **USB device** recommended.
