# NUCU Conduct and Health Requirements — 2026

The archived 2026 NUCU guide sets participation, conduct and hospital-visit requirements, including daily temperature checks, masks, attire and consent for recording. This page summarizes **the guide's 2026 rules and legal/health assertions**, not current law, medical advice or verified 2027 policy. Source pages 40–44 contain these rules; public-release approval was granted on 2026-09-28.

## Attendance, behavior and dismissal

Participants are expected to be mature, lawful, responsible and cooperative; respect university rules, property and attendance requirements; attend classes/activities, complete assignments, arrive punctually, wear clean clothes and keep shared places clean. The guide lists unauthorized absences, profane/lewd/indecent behavior, recurring tardiness/rudeness/“negativity” and university-rule violations as grounds for dismissal. Academic-staff dismissal means the participant cannot complete the program.

The guide asserts immediate dismissal for illegal activities without warning or second chances. Its examples include illegal-drug use, attempted purchase or possession, being with someone using/possessing illegal drugs, drug equipment/paraphernalia, unauthorized prescription drugs, misuse or sharing/selling prescription medicine, and misuse of over-the-counter drugs. It distinguishes permitted directed use of over-the-counter medicine.

Theft is described as removing public/private property, online or physically, without payment/consent, including another student's property; university theft brings direct disciplinary action. Endangerment includes threats, assault, fighting and jeopardizing safety, security or health. Verbal, physical or cyber bullying and sexual, racial or personal harassment are prohibited; respect for religion, ethnicity, culture, gender and sexual orientation is required.

## Alcohol, smoking and recording

The guide states a drinking age of **20**, bans driving **including bicycles** under alcohol's influence, describes strict intoxication standards and penalties, and warns about passengers knowingly riding with an intoxicated driver. These legal claims have not been independently checked here.

It says smoking is prohibited below **20**, and program rules prohibit smoking on campus and at off-campus program activities; all university buildings and program events are smoke-free. Photography, video or audio recording of a participant, administrator or professor requires that person's consent. Unauthorized recording, photographs or web postings are stated grounds for dismissal.

## Daily temperature and masks

Participants must bring a **thermometer** and measure temperature every morning. The source bars participation for a reading **higher than 37.5°C**, or **between 37°C and 37.5°C for two consecutive days**. It adds a qualification: if the standard differs greatly from a person's normal temperature, consider a significant increase over their normal temperature a fever. It does not precisely resolve threshold inclusivity or individual exceptions; do not invent a clinical interpretation.

The stated rationale is hospital visits and contact with patients/local residents. **Surgical masks are required on entering the hospital**; the packing list specifies masks for **at least five days**. These are archived source requirements, not a current medical protocol.

## Dress, grooming and tattoos

Hospital attire: pants/slacks or skirts **knee-length or longer**, shirts/blouses and **closed-toe shoes**. Campus dress should be casual but tasteful/appropriate. Jewelry should be minimal, safe and professional; facial piercings discreet and jewelry plain/flat. Makeup should be minimal; nails short/clean and any polish conservative. Hair should be clean/well groomed; clips safe and consistent with infection-control standards; hair color should not draw undue attention according to the guide.

The source asks for no perfume to avoid patient discomfort/allergic reactions, explicitly prohibiting **perfume/cologne May 18–23**. It requires tattoo coverage during the program with participant-provided body stickers/skin-colored patches where needed. Its broader assertion about tattoos in Japanese hot springs, pools and public baths is a generalization, not a verified rule for every venue.
