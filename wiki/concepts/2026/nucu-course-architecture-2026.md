# NUCU People-First Course Architecture — 2026

The archived 2026 Leeds article describes **AI & Automation for Tomorrow’s Societies (BAIM 3300)** as a people-first course offered through the **Management Information Systems program**. Its Build in Japan initiative joins CU Boulder and Nagoya University students in field research, cross-cultural teamwork and venture development. This page records the article's learning design, not a verified current syllabus or evidence that every activity produced a measured outcome.

## Seven-stage sequence

| Order | Stage | Contents in the article |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundation | Japan's historical, institutional and demographic transformation |
| 2 | Technology Primer | TCP/IP, OSI, workflow systems, automation, AI, LLMs, AI agents |
| 3 | Design Sprint | Ideation, product sketches, venture development |
| 4 | Global Collaboration | CU Boulder + Nagoya University teams |
| 5 | Field Validation | Hospitals, startups, robotics labs, elder-care facilities, rural communities |
| 6 | Venture Presentation | Final pitches at Idea Stoa at NIC, Nagoya University (reviewer correction) |
| 7 | Reflection & Microcredentials | Portable credentials, professional portfolios, future learning |

The prose adds learning about AI models, **SaaS platforms** and emerging technologies before international teams identify problems, research, prototype and validate ideas with stakeholders. The article's “Idea Store” label is an error: on 2026-09-29 the reviewer confirmed **Idea Stoa at NIC, Nagoya University** as the venue. The raw wording is preserved in [[leeds-build-in-japan-article-2026]].

## Why begin with people?

The central question is how technology should serve societies facing urgent challenges. The article contrasts **“What can AI build?”** with **“What should AI build?”** It uses Japan's aging population, labor shortages, healthcare demand, rural depopulation and social isolation to connect business decisions with ethics and human needs. Students are expected to discover the problem before choosing technology, and use AI to augment human capability rather than replace it.

The article reports **20 Leeds students** in spring 2026, partnering with **Ludus Labs**, during what it calls both **two weeks in Tokyo and Nagoya** and a **15-day experience**. These are source descriptions of the same experience, not two different programs. It presents the course as a global startup studio combining cultural learning, AI and entrepreneurship.

## Field evidence and venture development

The named visits are **Google for Startups**, **Techstars Tokyo**, **Nagoya University Hospital**, the university's **xR Center** and rural healthcare facilities in **Nakatsugawa**. Students engaged with founders, clinicians, researchers, caregivers and public-sector leaders. The xR Center, glossed as **AR/VR/MR**, suggested applications in medical training, patient communication and clinical teamwork. Rural visits connected demographic change with healthcare access, local economies and community life.

The venture-learning goals include identifying meaningful problems, testing assumptions, defining an addressable market, working across cultures and communicating with clarity and purpose. Not every concept is intended to become a company. See [[nucu-student-venture-portfolio-2026]] for the five named proposals and their intended users.

## Reflection and career readiness

The article presents **microcredentials and venture portfolios** as ways to demonstrate global entrepreneurship, design thinking, AI and automation, primary market research, venture pitching and cross-cultural collaboration. It supplies no credential award conditions or individual award evidence.

Anonymous reflections emphasize elderly people's and care workers' resilience, greater confidence through presentations, making a usable prototype and presenting it before a board, and understanding another culture's thinking. Another praises Professor Brady's lifelong-learning and ethical approach. These reports support the article's account of student experience; they are not independent measurements of learning gains.

The larger aim combines technological fluency with empathy, curiosity and responsible decisions. See [[nucu-startup-learning-2026]] for earlier program sources and [[leeds-build-in-japan-article-2026]] for author information, named perspectives and the unresolved July 1/July 2 publication date.
